Sales Performance Analysis & Dashboard

Project overview
This project delivers a multi-year sales performance evaluation analyzing transactional trends, volume shifts, unit pricing dynamics, and profitability across daily, monthly, and yearly intervals. Spanning four years of historical sales records (1,053 total transactions), the analysis investigates a macro peak in performance followed by continuous post-2014 contractions. By identifying operational efficiency gaps and day-of-week trade-offs, this initiative establishes a data-driven foundation for strategic sales recovery and margin optimization.

Business objective
The primary goal is to diagnose the root causes of declining post-2014 transaction volumes and total revenue. Specific objectives include evaluating pricing power stability over time, uncovering seasonal demand shifts throughout the year, analyzing weekday versus weekend sales behaviors, and pinpointing opportunities to widen gross profit margins without sacrificing volume.

What analysis focuses on
The analytical framework concentrates on four primary domains to evaluate overall commercial health. It tracks annual sales volume, cost of sales trajectories, and revenue growth cycles to establish broader macro trends. It assesses pricing and unit economics by examining average unit sales price fluctuations over time alongside the relationship between daily price per unit and quantity sold. Additionally, it evaluates intra-year seasonality through monthly sales totals and breaks down day-of-week behaviors to compare overall transaction volume against profitability metrics. Finally, it examines underlying cost structures and total versus daily gross profit percentages to evaluate overall operational efficiency.

Data preparation
Raw transaction data was processed using Databricks SQL to clean, aggregate, and structure datasets for analytical workflows. String parsing and type casting functions were applied to standardize raw date fields, numeric metrics, and currency columns into clean numeric types, specifically creating clean fields for total sales and cost of sales. Databricks SQL aggregations and conditional logic were leveraged to calculate derived operational metrics, including daily gross profit percentages, average unit sales prices, and day-of-week categorizations. These transformations were saved as structured views to directly power interactive dashboards across multiple visualization platforms without requiring intermediate local files.

Analytics and dashboards
The analysis was operationalized across several interactive dashboard environments, including Looker Studio, Databricks Dashboards, Power BI, Excel, and Lovable. Executive summary dashboards display top-level key performance indicators, highlighting 5.3 million total units sold, R186.9 million in total sales, and R194.0 million in total cost of sales across 1,053 records over four years. Time-series views illustrate annual quantity trends alongside monthly revenue fluctuations. Operational and pricing dashboards incorporate daily gross profit tables, weekday versus weekend volume distribution charts showing a 66.4% weekday and 33.6% weekend split, and scatter distributions plotting unit pricing against quantity sold.

Key analysis
The findings reveal a pronounced multi-year volume and revenue decline following a historical peak in 2014. Sales volume reached 2.3 million units in 2014—representing 43.26% of total historical volume—before declining by 26.1% in 2015 to 1.7 million units, and dropping further to 1.3 million units in 2016. Total annual revenue mirrored this movement, peaking near R75 million in 2014 before contracting to approximately R47 million by 2016. Average unit sales prices jumped from R74.1 in 2013 to R13.5K in 2014 and 2015, but softened to R11.9K in 2016, indicating eroding pricing power. Intra-year demand builds steadily through the first half of the year to peak in May at over R21 million, followed by a severe decline in July to around R12 million, a brief August recovery, and progressive weakening into November. Furthermore, day-of-week analysis uncovers a strong trade-off between volume and profitability: Saturday generates the highest overall sales revenue at R41.9 million, while Tuesday yields the highest daily gross profit margin at 48.68% despite having the lowest sales volume and unit price. Conversely, Monday generates the lowest gross profit margin at 44.88% despite commanding the highest average unit price.

Final presentation
The analytical findings, conclusions, and strategic recommendations were consolidated into an executive PowerPoint presentation. The presentation recommends investigating post-2014 root causes of volume loss, including market competition, distribution channels, and product mix, to halt top-line contraction. It advises protecting high-volume weekend sales while implementing targeted mid-week promotional strategies on high-margin days like Tuesday. Additionally, it highlights the need for targeted second-half promotional campaigns to counter the annual July-to-November drop-off, alongside closer cost-of-sales monitoring to negotiate better supplier terms and widen operating margins.
